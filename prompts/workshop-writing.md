# Workshop a piece of writing

Workshop a piece of writing with me, following the approach from <https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49753616>.

Input: $ARGUMENTS

Resolve the input as follows:

1. When it names a file, read that file as the piece. Any remaining text is the focus of the first pass.
2. When it is prose, treat it as the piece itself.
3. When it is empty, ask me for the piece and stop.

The piece is mine to change. Keep every file untouched; all of your output goes into the conversation.

## Rules

- Give suggested corrections only. Leave out encouragement, praise, and summaries of what works, because only suggested corrections are useful to me.
- Describe what should change and why, without supplying wording. Any words you write are disqualified from the piece, so a good phrase from you is a phrase I can no longer use. This covers replacement sentences, example rephrasings, alternative titles, and "something like …" drafts.
- Assume I know my audience extremely well. Skip advice about who the readers are or what they will tolerate.
- Note typos briefly and move on. Assume I meant to write properly, and keep typos out of any structural critique.

## Passes

We work in a series of passes, usually 8 to 12 for one piece. Each pass has one focus, such as structure, argument, paragraph flow, sentence rhythm, or word choice.

1. When I named a focus, use it. Otherwise, propose the focus for the first pass in one line and proceed with it.
2. Keep every suggestion locked to the current pass. Hold observations that belong to another pass until we get there.
3. Anchor each suggestion to its location by quoting the few words of mine that it concerns.
4. End the pass after the last suggestion and wait for me. I will reply with the revised piece, a new focus, or both.

Expect me to act on roughly 60% of suggestions. I decline the rest because I wanted the text to sound that way, and I often rewrite a whole paragraph instead of applying a suggestion as given. Treat a declined suggestion as settled and leave it out of later passes. Re-read the piece at the start of each pass, since earlier suggestions may no longer apply.
